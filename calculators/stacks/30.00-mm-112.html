Shop use

30 mm, a common spacer and height-gage check. The 112-piece set has no 30 mm block. Its long blocks are 25, 50, 75, and 100 mm.

How this was chosen

The digit-by-digit method starts with the block that clears the right-hand digits, then each following block clears the next place until nothing is left. Every extra block adds a wrung joint, so the fewest-block stack gives the least stack-up error and the least handling.

25 mm is the first long block and 5 mm is in the 0.5 mm series, 0.5 mm through 24.5 mm. One joint. 24.5 mm plus something else is a longer stack, and there is no 5.5 mm block. An 88-piece set does contain a 30 mm block, in its 10 mm series. This page stays on the 112-piece set.

  1. 25 mm. Stack so far 25 mm, and what is left to build, 5 mm, is a whole number.
  2. 5 mm. Stack so far 30 mm, which completes the stack.

For a steel stack of 30 mm the length changes by about 0.34 micrometre for every 1 °C (1.8 °F) away from the 20 °C (68 °F) reference. Typical steel coefficient 11.5 × 10⁻⁶ per °C.

FAQ

Can I mix inch and metric blocks to make 30 mm?

No. This recipe uses only the 112-piece metric set. 30 mm stays in millimetres. Change the set in the calculator if your blocks are the other unit.

Do I need wear blocks for 30 mm?

Not for this recipe. The stack is the measuring blocks only (25 + 5). For a stack that will be wrung often, see the wear-block guide and put a wear block on each end.

How do I wring the 30 mm stack?

Clean the faces and wring thickest first: 25 + 5. Slide each face together. Do not push a nicked face straight down onto the stack.

What if one of these blocks is out of the set?

Open the calculator on this size and uncheck the missing block. It searches again. The combination on this page uses each listed block once and adds to 30 mm.

Is 2 blocks the shortest stack for 30 mm?

Yes for the 112-piece metric set with every block available. 25 + 5 sums to 30 mm in the set’s integer units.
