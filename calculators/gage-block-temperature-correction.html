Gage Block Temperature Correction

A gage block's stated length is its length at 20 °C (68 °F). Warm the block and it grows. The change is

ΔL = L × α × ΔT

ΔT is the block temperature minus 20 °C. α is the coefficient of linear expansion per °C. The length at the shop temperature is L + ΔL. This is the block's own change. It is not automatically the error in a measurement.

Coefficients

These are the nominal values at 20 °C in NIST Monograph 180, Table 3.2. A certificate may list a measured coefficient for that set. Use the measured value when you have it. The presets are a starting point.

Materialα (10⁻⁶ / °C)
Steel gage block, under 25 mm11.5
Ceramic gage block (zirconia)9.2
Chrome carbide8.4
Tungsten carbide4.5

NIST lists a 500 mm steel gage block at 10.6 × 10⁻⁶ / °C, because the core of a long steel block is not hardened the same way as the ends. ISO 3650 specifies (11.5 ± 1.0) × 10⁻⁶ per K for steel gage blocks. Do not apply 11.5 to a long steel block without looking at the certificate.

Worked example

A 4.000 in steel block at 23 °C. ΔT is 3 °C. α is 11.5 × 10⁻⁶ per °C.

ΔL = 4.000 × 11.5×10⁻⁶ × 3 = 0.000138 in

That is 138 millionths of an inch. The block is about 4.000138 in long at 23 °C. The form above starts on this example. Open the 4.000 in block for the nominal stack, which is the single 4 inch block. The nominal stack does not include this temperature change.

NIST's own illustration of the same coefficient: a 100 mm steel block grows 1.15 µm when it warms 1 °C, because 100 × 11.5×10⁻⁶ = 0.00115 mm.

If the part is also steel at 11.5 × 10⁻⁶, both grow together and the first-order difference is zero. If the blocks are tungsten carbide (4.5 × 10⁻⁶) and the part is steel (11.5 × 10⁻⁶), a 100 mm length and a 5 °C rise differ by

ΔL = 100 × (11.5 − 4.5)×10⁻⁶ × 5 = 0.0035 mm

That 0.0035 mm is 3.5 µm. Enter the part coefficient in the form to see the difference for your own length. Leave it blank to see only the block.

FAQ

What temperature is a gage block length stated at?

20 °C, which is 68 °F. ASME B89.1.9 uses that reference temperature. A nominal size is the length at 20 °C, not the length at shop temperature.

What coefficient should I use for steel blocks?

The nominal value for steel gage blocks is 11.5 × 10⁻⁶ per °C. NIST Monograph 180 lists 11.5 for a steel gage block under 25 mm, and 10.6 for a 500 mm steel block. ISO 3650 specifies (11.5 ± 1.0) × 10⁻⁶ per K for steel. Use the certificate value when you have one.

Does this correction matter if the part is steel too?

The block grows, and a steel part with the same coefficient grows by about the same amount. The difference between them can be near zero even though each one changed. The calculator on this page reports the block change. The difference is L × (α_part − α_block) × ΔT, and only when you enter a part coefficient.
