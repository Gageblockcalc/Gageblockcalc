Worked example

  1. Convert the angle. 10° is already decimal degrees. Degrees, minutes, and seconds convert as degrees + minutes/60 + seconds/3600.
  2. Multiply by the sine. A 5 in sine bar at 10° has H = 5 × sin(10°) = 0.86824089 in.
  3. Round to the set increment. An 81-piece inch set steps by 0.0001 in. 0.86824089 in rounds to 0.8682 in, which is 0.00004089 in under the exact height. That is inside the ±0.00005 in half-step.

FAQ

What formula does a sine bar use?

Stack height H = L × sin(angle), with the angle in degrees. Minutes and seconds convert first: decimal degrees = degrees + minutes/60 + seconds/3600.

Why is the gage block stack not exactly L × sin(angle)?

Gage blocks move in fixed steps. The 81-piece inch set’s smallest step is 0.0001 in. The 88-piece metric set’s smallest repeating step is 0.001 mm. One 1.0005 mm block can finish a single 0.0005 mm ending, but that is not a 0.0005 mm step for every height, so this page rounds metric heights to 0.001 mm. The stack matches the rounded height. The difference from the exact sine height is at most half a step: 0.00005 in or 0.0005 mm.
