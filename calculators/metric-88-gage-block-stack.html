Metric 88-Piece Gage Block Stack Calculator

Find which blocks from a Mitutoyo-style 88-piece metric set wring together to a target length in millimetres, using as few blocks as possible. The long series steps by 10 mm (10–100), and the 0.5 mm series stops at 9.5 mm.

Formula

Target = b₁ + b₂ + … + bₙ (minimise n)
88-pc series: 0.001 mm (1.001–1.009) → 0.01 mm (1.01–1.49) → 0.5 mm (0.5–9.5) → 10 mm (10–100)

Worked example

  1. Clear the thousandths. 41.125 ends in 5 in the 3rd place, so take a 1.005 mm block. Remainder: 41.125 − 1.005 = 40.120.
  2. Clear the hundredths. Take 1.12 mm. Remainder: 40.120 − 1.12 = 39.000.
  3. Finish with the 88-piece long blocks. The 10 mm series includes 30, and the 0.5 mm series includes 9. 39.000 = 30 + 9. Done.
1.005 + 1.12 + 9 + 30 = 41.125 mm — 4 blocks.

FAQ

What is in a typical 88-piece metric gage block set?

A Mitutoyo Series 516-style 88-piece set (1 mm base) includes 1.0005 mm, 1.001–1.009 mm, 1.01–1.49 mm, 0.5–9.5 mm in 0.5 mm steps, and 10–100 mm in 10 mm steps.

How is an 88-piece set different from a 112-piece set?

The thousandths and hundredths series match. The 112-piece set continues the 0.5 mm series to 24.5 mm and uses 25, 50, 75, and 100 mm for the long blocks. The 88-piece set stops the 0.5 mm series at 9.5 mm and steps the long blocks by 10 mm, so the same target often uses a different pair of larger blocks.

Can every metric target be made with an 88-piece set?

No. Targets below the smallest block, or with more decimal places than 0.0005 mm, cannot be built exactly. When no combination exists, the calculator says so. Switch to the 112-piece set for steps the 10 mm series cannot make on its own.
