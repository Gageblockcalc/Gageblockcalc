Formula

Target = b₁ + b₂ + … + bₙ (minimise n)
112-pc series: 0.001 mm (1.001–1.009) → 0.01 mm (1.01–1.49) → 0.5 mm (0.5–24.5) → long blocks (25, 50, 75, 100)

Worked example

  1. Clear the thousandths. 41.125 ends in 5 in the 3rd place, so take a 1.005 mm block. Remainder: 41.125 − 1.005 = 40.120.
  2. Clear the hundredths. Take 1.12 mm. Remainder: 40.120 − 1.12 = 39.000.
  3. Finish with the 112-piece long blocks. The long series includes 25, and the 0.5 mm series includes 14. 39.000 = 25 + 14. Done.
1.005 + 1.12 + 14 + 25 = 41.125 mm — 4 blocks.

FAQ

What is in a typical 112-piece metric gage block set?

A Mitutoyo Series 516-style 112-piece set (1 mm base) includes 1.0005 mm, 1.001–1.009 mm, 1.01–1.49 mm, 0.5–24.5 mm in 0.5 mm steps, and 25, 50, 75, and 100 mm.

How is a 112-piece set different from an 88-piece set?

The thousandths and hundredths series match. The 112-piece set continues the 0.5 mm series to 24.5 mm and uses 25, 50, 75, and 100 mm for the long blocks. The 88-piece set stops the 0.5 mm series at 9.5 mm and steps the long blocks by 10 mm. On this set, 14 mm is one block; the 88-piece solver builds 14 mm as 10 + 4. The 88-piece blocks sum to 716.2955 mm, so a longer target has to use the 112-piece set, whose blocks sum to 933.7955 mm.

Can every metric target be made with a 112-piece set?

No. Targets below the smallest block, 0.5 mm, or with more decimal places than 0.0005 mm, cannot be built exactly. When no combination exists, the calculator says so.
