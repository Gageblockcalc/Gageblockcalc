Knoop Case Depth Calculator

A Knoop microhardness traverse walks indentations in from the part edge until the filar drum reaches a stop you set. This page turns the stage reading and the drum reading into distance from the edge, an effective case depth, the long diagonal, and an approximate HK. It runs in the browser. Nothing is uploaded.

Calculated HK is approximate. Your shop's conversion table or the part spec governs.

Worked check

The form opens on this check. Scale 190 filar units = 95 µm, so each unit is 0.5 µm. A drum reading of 190 is a 95 µm long diagonal. At 500 g:

HK = 14229 × 500 / 95² = 788.3

That is the same result as 14.229 × 0.5 kgf / (0.095 mm)². The default stop is 229 units, and 190 is below it, so the status says to keep going. The next stage reading is the current mark plus 0.002 in when the mark is at or above the zero. Set the drum to 229 or more and the status changes to stop reached. With the zero at 0.0000 and the mark at 0.0020, the distance is 0.0020 in and the effective case depth is 0.0030 in.

How to run the traverse

  1. Zero the stage on the part edge and enter that reading.
  2. Make the first mark about 0.002 in in from the edge. That drum reading is the near-surface case hardness.
  3. Step 0.001 to 0.003 in deeper for each mark and stagger the marks sideways.
  4. Keep the Knoop long axis parallel to the edge.
  5. Keep at least 2.5 times the short diagonal between marks and from the edge (ASTM E384).
  6. Enter the filar drum reading. Keep going until it reaches the stop, then read the effective case depth.

This page does not measure the short diagonal, and it does not apply a hardness conversion or a case-depth specification. Use the table and the spec your shop uses.

FAQ

How is HK calculated here?

HK = 14229 × load in grams ÷ (long diagonal in micrometres)². The same number is 14.229 × load in kilogram-force ÷ (long diagonal in millimetres)². A 500 g load and a 95 µm diagonal give about 788.3. Calculated HK is approximate. Your shop's conversion table or the part spec governs.

What does this page report as effective case depth?

The distance from the part edge to the mark, plus 0.001 in. Distance is the absolute difference of the mark stage reading and the zero reading. The added 0.001 in is the rule this page uses. It is not taken from a part specification. Record the case depth your shop procedure or the part spec tells you to record.

When does the calculator say to stop?

You set a stop in filar-drum units. The default is 229. A larger drum reading is a longer diagonal and a softer impression. While the drum reading is below the stop, the page says to keep going and names the next stage reading about 0.002 in further from the edge, in the same direction the mark already moved from the zero. If the mark is still on the zero, that step is in the positive direction. When the drum reading is at or above the stop, the page reports the effective case depth for that mark.

How should the marks be spaced?

Put the first mark about 0.002 in in from the edge. That reading is the near-surface hardness, not the case depth. Step about 0.001 to 0.003 in deeper for each later mark and stagger the marks sideways. Keep the Knoop long axis parallel to the edge. ASTM E384 calls for at least 2.5 times the short diagonal between marks and between a mark and the edge. This page does not measure the short diagonal.
