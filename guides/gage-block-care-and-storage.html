Gage Block Care and Storage

Gage blocks stay the length on the certificate only if the faces stay clean, flat, and free of rust. Most of the damage in a shop set is fingerprints, a stack left wrung, and a burr that someone kept using.

Steps

  1. Wipe before and after. Use a clean, lint-free cloth. A chip on one face becomes a scratch on the next block you wring to it.
  2. Oil steel when it goes away. After handling, wipe a light rust-preventive film on steel faces and put the block in its pocket. Before the next wring, wipe that film back off until only a trace remains. A wet face will not wring to the block length. The wringing page is the trace-of-oil step.
  3. Do not oil ceramic or carbide to stop rust. They do not rust the way steel does. Wipe them so the faces are clean. A dirty ceramic face scratches just as a dirty steel face does.
  4. Take stacks apart. Slide blocks apart. Do not pry. Store them unwrung, in the case, closed, away from chips, coolant, and a window that heats the box.
  5. Stone only a raised burr. If a fingernail catches, a few light passes on a clean stone can drop the burr. Stop when the nail no longer catches. A stone is not a way to "true up" a worn block.
  6. Let heat leave before a tight length. The stated length is at 20 °C (68 °F). A block just out of a warm hand is long. The temperature calculator shows how much.
  7. Keep wear on the wear blocks. When a stack touches a part or a plate, the sacrificial blocks go on the outside. The wear-block page and the 2.3456 in wear recipe show how the calculator includes them.

Worked habit

You built 0.8682 in as 0.650 + 0.118 + 0.1002 for a sine bar, used it, and now the job is done.

  1. Slide 0.1002 off, then slide 0.118 off 0.650.
  2. Wipe each steel face. If the set is steel, put a light film on for the case.
  3. Set each block in the pocket marked for that size. Close the case.
  4. Next time, wipe the film off before you wring. Open 0.8682 in again only after the faces are ready.

Calibration is a separate decision. A daily working set and a master that stays in the lab do not share one calendar. This site does not invent an interval. FAQ

Should steel blocks be oiled in the case?

Yes. After you handle steel, wipe on a light rust-preventive film before the blocks go back in the case. Wipe that film off before the next wring. A thick film adds length and stops a good wring. Ceramic and carbide do not rust the way steel does. They still need to be wiped clean so grit does not sit on the face.

Can I leave a stack wrung together in the box?

No. Take the stack apart by sliding, then oil steel faces and put each block in its pocket. A stack left wrung can be hard to separate, and steel can corrode in the joint.

How often should blocks be calibrated?

The interval belongs to your quality system and to any customer requirement. There is no single interval that fits a master set and a daily working set. The calibration guide on Ask a Machinist is the longer discussion. This calculator does not pick an interval.
