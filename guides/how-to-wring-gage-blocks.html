How to Wring Gage Blocks

Wringing is how two gage blocks become one length. The faces are flat and smooth enough that a trace of oil and a sliding twist make them hold. The joint should nearly disappear. If you can see a thick line, the blocks are not wrung. They are sitting on oil or dirt.

Steps

  1. Clean the measuring faces. Wipe both faces with a clean, lint-free cloth. A light solvent such as mineral spirits is a common shop wipe. Let the faces dry. Grit left on a face will not wring, and it can scratch the block.
  2. Stone only a raised burr. If a fingernail catches a nick, a clean gage block stone can knock that burr down. A few light passes are enough. Stoning is not a way to clean a face or to change the size.
  3. Leave only a trace of oil. A dry, freshly solvent-wiped face often will not wring. The usual film is the oil from clean skin, or a drop of very light oil wiped almost all the way off. A wet face wrings on the oil, and that joint is not the block length.
  4. Cross, slide, and twist. Set one face across the other, slide them under light pressure, and twist until the faces are in line. A good wring holds the lower block and the joint line nearly disappears. Do not hit the blocks together.
  5. Separate by sliding. Slide the blocks apart. Prying with a tool chips an edge. Do not leave a stack wrung for days. A long wring can be hard to separate, and steel faces can corrode in the joint.

Worked example

A 5 inch sine bar at 10° needs a stack of 0.8682 in on an 81-piece set. The solver, with wear blocks off, builds that size as 0.650 + 0.118 + 0.1002. Those three blocks sum to 0.8682 in.

  1. Clean all six measuring faces. There are two faces on each of the three blocks.
  2. Wring 0.650 to 0.118. The calculator lists the thickest block first, and that is a good wring order.
  3. Wring 0.1002 onto the free face of 0.118.
  4. The stack should hold together when you lift the 0.650 block. If 0.1002 falls off, wipe that face and wring it again.

FAQ

Why will clean blocks not wring?

A face that was just washed dry, or a face with a burr or a speck of grit, will not wring. Wipe again, stone only a raised nick, and leave a trace of oil, not a wet film.

Does the length of a block include a wring?

Yes. The standardized length of a gage block includes the effect of one wring. A stack of nominal sizes is the shop length. When the last tenth matters, use the deviations on the calibration certificate instead of the nominal sizes alone.

Should wear blocks be wrung on the outside?

Yes, when the stack will touch a part, an anvil, or a surface plate. The two wear blocks go on the ends so the contact wears them, not the set. The calculator can include them when the link has wear=1.
